# Baseline policy: defensible comparisons and deferred calibration

Research date: 2026-10-27. Decision proposal for [When is a usual obtainable price defensible?](tickets/baseline-policy.md). Parent review owns acceptance and tracker changes. This asset changes no ticket, map or implementation.

## Recommended planning decision

Accept four separate, evidence-qualified outputs: usual obtainable price, current best comparable offer, dated observed historical low, and personal eligible price. Adopt the cohort, coverage, exclusion, correction and explanation requirements below. **Defer the estimator, numerical time window, collection cadence, minimum evidence counts, freshness limits, unusual-deal threshold and statistical change-detection parameters.** The retained evidence cannot discriminate these choices. A conventional rolling median, a minimum number of rows, or a familiar sale-calendar window would supply false precision.

No retained watch series currently qualifies for a usual UK delivered-price baseline. No current-best UK delivered total or public-coupon success is certified by this report. Limited evidence must produce an explicit unavailable baseline and no percentage saving. Current-best and observed-low claims can eventually qualify independently of usual-price sufficiency, but only within their own checked coverage and evidence gates. This decision is sufficient for downstream explanation design, with unavailable states; it is not empirical acceptance of pricing or the complete watch workflow.

## External constraints and what they do not decide

CMA guidance says the DMCC unfair-commercial-practices provisions apply from 6 April 2025 and cover misleading information about price calculation and a specific price advantage. This is the current published framework relevant to future commercial claims, not a finding that this private planning exercise is a trader or that the service has passed legal review. [CMA, Unfair commercial practices, overview and misleading actions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices).

CAP's current savings guidance assesses reference-price recency, duration, pricing history, sales evidence and channel. It expects at least three months of pricing history for assessing an advertised usual selling price; recurring reductions and prolonged promotions can undermine a higher reference. This is advertising substantiation guidance, **not a validated three-month estimator window for PriceRecon**. Offered prices do not establish completed transactions. We lack merchant sales data; a sampled obtainable-offer summary must never be labelled the merchant's usual selling price or a substantiated merchant “was” saving. [ASA/CAP, Promotional savings claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/promotional-savings-claims.html). RRP is also not proven by a manufacturer's recommendation alone. [ASA/CAP, Recommended retail prices](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recommended-retail-prices-rrp.html).

ONS treats sampling as necessary and acknowledges sampling error; its current traditional-data methodology explicitly describes outlet and product selection. This supports declaring the sampling frame rather than claiming universal coverage. It supplies neither a watch-deal sample size nor appropriate seller weights for this single buyer. [ONS, Traditional data aggregates in consumer prices](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/methodologies/traditionaldataaggregatesinconsumerprices).

The older ONS technical manual distinguishes sale, stock absence and non-comparable replacement, but excludes coupons/loyalty cards and treats delivery separately. Its rules serve inflation measurement, not this delivered-price buying decision. Borrow the discipline of labelled states, not its cadence, imputation rules, coupon exclusion or validation percentages. [ONS, Consumer Prices Indices Technical Manual 2019, sections 6 and 9](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/methodologies/consumerpricesindicestechnicalmanual2019).

## Four outputs and their independent gates

**Usual obtainable price:** a recent, scoped account of repeatedly available comparable public purchase opportunities, including evidenced routine sales and public codes. Its target population is the declared supported merchant/offer frame for this exact buying scenario, not all watches, all sellers, transactions, or RRP. Whether one scalar adequately describes that population is an empirical question. A range or separately labelled promotion regimes may be more honest. Method, window, included observations, gaps, weights, exclusions and policy version must be inspectable. Until calibrated: “Usual obtainable price unavailable — insufficient comparable history.”

**Current best comparable offer:** the lowest qualified payable total among fresh, available, exact comparable offers actually checked, at stated times and eligibility. There need not be history, but every candidate must pass identity and UK-cost gates. If some supported routes failed, say “lowest verified total among the offers checked” with failed/capped routes prominent; withhold a stronger whole-roster claim. An unknown or conditional total cannot silently lose a numeric sort to a verified total and imply it is more expensive. Show it outside the verified ranking. Auction bids, expired prices and stale stock are excluded. Equal totals may tie; convenience/risk preferences can rank recommendations separately, without changing which price is lowest. Freshness remains deferred pending measured volatility and recheck costs.

**Observed dated historical low:** the minimum qualified retained observation in a named window, cohort and eligibility basis, carrying observation date, offer, cost basis and coverage/gaps. It is neither a continuously obtainable price nor an all-time/global record. A single qualifying past observation can support only “lowest of these retained observations” with the one-observation limit obvious; it cannot support “record deal” or a usual-price saving. More intensive sampling tends to create more opportunities to see a low, so comparisons across differently sampled windows require disclosure. Known obsolete offers may remain historical evidence, never current buyable prices. A disputed low is withheld pending adjudication; dependent outputs are invalidated after correction.

**Personal eligible price:** an exact buyer scenario, separately labelled verified at observation time, conditional, or unknown under the discount/cost contracts. Public discovery of a code does not make membership, new-customer, app, coins, payment or redemption conditions universal. Compare a personal opportunity to an equally eligible personal history only when such history exists; otherwise show the public comparator beside it with the eligibility difference, not a homogeneous public saving. Cashback remains uncertain later benefit; gift cards/credit preserve acquisition/tender context. No personal account eligibility is inferred here and no portal integration is approved.

Even when a baseline later qualifies, describe any arithmetic as a difference from the stated sampled obtainable-price basis, not retailer-advertised savings. Baseline and candidate must have identical units, currency/payment basis and eligibility. A percentage is withheld for an unqualified/nonpositive denominator or unstable basis. A below-budget delivered alert can qualify without a usual-price baseline only after its independent exact-total, availability, freshness and user-rule gates pass.

## Cohort and eligibility contract

Consume the versioned observation and correction contract from [history](observation-history-research.md), [identity](identity-evidence-research.md), [UK cost](uk-cost-research.md) and [discounts](public-discounts-research.md); do not create a competing evidence schema. A comparison policy identifies:

- Exact reference/revision, movement, size, dial, strap/bracelet and complete-watch status; selected merchant SKU and evidence version. A parent PID, title or cheapest accessory option is insufficient. Variant changes split series unless accepted evidence proves continuity; old data is never silently relabelled.
- Condition, quantity/bundle, purchase mode and fulfilment commitment. New, refurbished and used stay separate. Used comparison also requires condition/accessory/warranty descriptors; unique-item asking prices and auctions cannot become a new-watch baseline. Sparse used cohorts may remain descriptive indefinitely. Listings ending do not prove sales or achieved prices.
- UK destination scope, dispatch route, shipping service, tax/import treatment, full payable components, currency and payment FX context. Unknown components stay unknown; conditional totals form separate scenarios. Preserve native prices to distinguish FX changes from merchant repricing. Do not splice a new cost methodology into old totals without a versioned recomputation.
- Eligibility and channel: ordinary public purchase, reproducible public code scenario, and restricted/personal scenarios. Whole-basket thresholds and filler purchases remain full commitments, not an allocated watch saving hiding extra spend. Compare basket scenarios only where commitments are equivalent; show item allocation separately.

Routine public sales/codes belong in the obtainable history when their exact application is evidenced. Excluding every “sale” would inflate the baseline; including every advertised banner would fabricate availability. Retain no-code and applied-code alternatives with common lineage; for a public opportunity estimand they are alternative prices for one purchase opportunity, not independent votes. Coupon unavailability, expiry, redemption failure and absent verification remain distinct. Never subtract a code from an already discounted observed total again.

## Candidate methods to evaluate, not select now

A method must first say what “usual” means. Pooling every seller observation estimates the typical sampled listing; summarising the cheapest qualifying offer in each observation period estimates the price a buyer could find within checked coverage. Neither interpretation automatically equals a usual obtainable market price. Assess both, make the chosen target explicit, and avoid a high-listing-price population making a routinely available cheaper seller look unusually good.

1. **Unweighted pooled mean/median of quotes.** Transparent diagnostic benchmark. The mean can be pulled by extreme values; the median avoids dependence on their magnitude but cannot repair duplicate merchants, selection bias, eligibility mixing or a two-regime market. Repeated polling of one shop dominates either method. Reject as an automatic production default.
2. **Period-balanced seller summaries, then a cross-period summary.** Reduces the vote from oversampling and preserves seller diversity. Requires justified period boundaries and weighting; equal merchant weights can overvalue obscure/unavailable sellers. Duration weighting needs observed support and must not carry a price through failed gaps. Evaluate against acquisition-period balance, not inferred continuous availability.
3. **Distribution of each period's best qualified public total.** Closest candidate to a buyer willing to compare supported sellers. Sensitive to changing coverage, transient stock and a single unreliable cheap merchant. Missing cheap sellers can inflate it; adding a merchant can lower it without any repricing. Report those coverage changes rather than treating them as market shifts.
4. **Lower quantile or trimmed summary.** Could represent regularly attainable lower-end opportunities while resisting isolated extremes. Quantile/trim choices encode a buyer opportunity frequency and may discard genuine rare deals or preserve misleading highs. Parameters require measured recurrence and buyer acceptance, not a customary percentile.
5. **Duration/mode or promotion-regime description.** Useful if ordinary pricing and recurring sale/code states differ. Exact-price modes fragment with FX and minor edits; grouping tolerances and promotion-duration evidence are needed. A regime distribution may be preferable to an unjustified scalar. Never treat advertised campaign dates as proof of continuous stock.
6. **Recency-weighted or segmented baseline.** Can adapt to permanent changes, FX, new generations or clearance, but can follow a transient sale too quickly and erase its useful contrast. Requires backtesting detection delay, false shifts, reset/requalification and missing-data behavior. A historical minimum is retained as its own output, not adopted as usual price.

NIST distinguishes flagging, investigating and accommodating outliers: unusual points may be errors or valuable real events. Verify variant, quantity, decimal/currency, cost, coupon and stock evidence before excluding a cheap price; preserve valid exceptional lows. Robust summaries do not establish provenance. [NIST, Detection of outliers](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35h.htm). Repeated time samples can be dependent; row counts cannot be interpreted as independent information. Standard autocorrelation also assumes equally spaced observations, which missing merchant acquisitions do not automatically satisfy. [NIST, Autocorrelation](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35c.htm).

## Bias, timing and change controls

Deduplicate discovery origins by attributable merchant offer/selected SKU and acquisition lineage; affiliate/search/community mirrors are leads to one opportunity. Preserve genuinely different seller/fulfilment/eligibility scenarios even when titles coincide. One seller's multiple storefronts may share price setting; record known links, leave unknown independence explicit and evaluate grouped sensitivity. Unknown seller identity cannot count toward an independence claim.

Retain planned query/page/category coverage, caps, rank position, pagination, discovery origin and acquisition outcomes before price/preference filtering. Top-ranked search results, affiliate-accessible sellers and successful endpoints are a selected sample. Do not use click/popularity as a substitute for transaction weights. A cheapness-filtered history cannot independently establish that offers are cheap. Evaluate a declared panel revisited irrespective of price alongside new category discovery; disclose supported-route limits and merchant turnover. Single-brand exclusive distribution cannot manufacture multiple-seller diversity; restrict any baseline to that merchant/channel.

Scheduled sampling and promotion-triggered verification serve different purposes. Preserve both but do not let intensified sale polling overweight sale duration. Fixed times can miss short codes; calendar-only promotion checks can miss ordinary discounts. Future cadence experiments must cover different times/days, observed campaign transitions and non-campaign periods within route rights/operating limits. Source publication, capture, persistence, advertised start/end and actual validated application times remain separate with timezone. A cache replay is not a fresh capture. Between valid captures, a transition time is interval-bounded, not known exactly.

Out-of-stock, ended, unavailable shipping, preorder and unknown states are not interchangeable. A failed acquisition is an unknown period, not an expensive offer, vanished merchant or observed zero. No forward fill across gaps for obtainable-price duration. Exclude stale/unbuyable offers from current ranking while preserving historical status. A current out-of-stock cheapest offer must not set a supposedly obtainable low today.

Market-shift detection must separate genuine same-cohort repricing from seller-entry/exit, source failure, query/ranking change, tax/FX/payment changes, coupon eligibility, identity correction and variant revision. Initially use explicit evidence review and show “baseline under review”; retain the prior baseline with dates rather than silently updating it. Candidate statistical detectors include rolling distribution comparisons, segmented regimes and CUSUM, with calibrated false-alarm/delay trade-offs. NIST's CUSUM description requires a reference process, variance and design parameters; its process-control performance is not proof for a sparse, dependent watch-offer sample. [NIST, CUSUM control charts](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc323.htm). No automatic reset threshold is accepted here.

## Concrete collection and evaluation acceptance plan

This is future work specification, not permission to collect or operate services.

1. **Admission and scope.** Owner records permission/retention-cleared routes and a dated cohort/source/eligibility manifest under the existing source and history contracts. Begin with the [qualified source roster](source-qualification-decision.md) and contrasting retained watch cases. Resolve ML05/ML05B identity; use AD2066 colour and SN0144 size/accessory conflicts as exclusion cases, exact Casio for retailer coverage, and Merkur unresolved for abstention. Include a genuinely qualified used case separately. A convenient exact item cannot stand in for the category population.
2. **Collection design before collection.** With operating-envelope ownership, specify an affordable reference schedule and revisit panel, source-specific permitted limits, time-of-day rotation, ordinary and campaign checks, failed-run recording and stopping rules. Select pilot schedule values as explicit experimental choices, not accepted thresholds. Record the rationale and revise if the schedule cannot distinguish actual promotion durations. Do not announce a fixed end date as evidence sufficiency.
3. **Evidence ledger and adjudication.** Collect initial, unchanged, increased and decreased prices and outcomes independently of alerts. Reconcile exact selection, destination, stock, complete cost and discounts. Record exclusions and manual adjudication evidence, uncertainty and correction lineage. Include verified no-coupon opportunities; do not wait for a coupon to create ordinary history. Claimed successful coupons require the separately authorised reproduction protocol. No purchase is needed to observe an offer; no observation is labelled a completed sale.
4. **Coverage and recurrence checkpoint.** Inspect elapsed periods, independent captures, merchant groups, time/day coverage, gaps, promotion/non-promotion episodes, code eligibility and duration bounds. Require repeated qualified opportunities across relevant states before trying to infer recurrence. For a seller-exclusive or still-sparse cohort, narrow the claim or continue abstaining. Counts, duration and required diversity are selected only after seeing volatility/coverage and the decision error they control; large raw counts alone never pass.
5. **Candidate comparison.** Freeze a versioned training period and evaluate candidates on later chronological observations with no future leakage. Keep the current candidate event from establishing its own comparator; document any later rolling update. Compare explanations against independently adjudicated recurring opportunities and temporary versus persistent changes, not against seller RRP. Report error in predicting later available comparable totals, unsupported “unusual” decisions, missed opportunities, ranking reversals, abstention rate and detection delay. Disclose that future-price prediction is evidence about usefulness, not the definition of usual price.
6. **Sensitivity and falsification.** Sweep plausible windows and cadences chosen from observed episode lengths; downsample a permissible reference series to estimate missed short offers. Compare period/seller balancing, pooled and period-best targets, code/no-code contexts, recency weighting and alternative outlier decisions. Leave out each merchant/group, promotion episode and discovery route; simulate documented gaps, duplicate leads, stale stock and corrections through offline analysis only. Track changes in baseline amount, deal status, current-best ranking and coverage. Use time/merchant blocks for uncertainty analysis if justified by dependence; do not issue an IID confidence interval from raw polls. Instability or disagreement on whether a deal exists is a reason to withhold a percentage or show regimes, not average methods into false certainty.
7. **Decision record and acceptance.** Before final tuning, the parent/buyer sets acceptable decision errors, missed-short-offer costs, freshness risk, explanation clarity and maintenance budget from the pilot evidence. The research evaluator reports the trade-off frontier and recommends parameter values with supporting periods/cases, uncertainty and failure modes. Acceptance requires traceable included/excluded observations, reproducible offline calculations, holdout results, sensitivity results, no fabricated evidence, and buyer review of representative explanations. If no method meets the agreed tolerances, accept a narrower claim or continue deferral. No numerical acceptance tolerance is invented in this report.
8. **Requalification.** Version method and evidence policy; retain prior assessments and invalidate descendants on identity/cost/coupon corrections. Review when merchant mix, access, volatility, eligibility, currency/cost basis or configuration changes. Calibrate detector/reset parameters only with adjudicated stable, temporary-sale and persistent-shift episodes and holdout evaluation. Record the next evidence checkpoint and owner rather than silently promoting a provisional pilot method.

The operating-envelope ticket owns feasible collection breadth/cadence, rights, costs and source freshness. Baseline research owns how those samples support a claim. Watch proof owns actual end-to-end evidence, while experience acceptance owns whether the buyer distinguishes these outputs. Deferral does not move empirical acceptance into a mockup or close those gates.

## Explanation and parent-review checklist

Each eventual assessment needs configuration/condition/quantity/purchase mode; destination and cost status; public or personal eligibility; observation age; checked merchants/routes and gaps; baseline method/window/status; dated observed low and scope; included/excluded evidence and correction version. Show seller RRP/banner as attributed claims if useful, visually separate from the measured comparison. Show whole-basket commitment and conditional benefits beside their amounts.

Example wording is a template, not a real offer: “Potential deal. Usual obtainable price is unavailable because comparable history is insufficient. This is the lowest verified total among the named offers checked at the shown times; other routes failed. Historical low unavailable. The member-code scenario is conditional and excluded from the public comparison.” Omit the lowest-total clause unless that independent gate actually passes. If history later qualifies, show the sampled period and eligibility beside its amount and explain whether routine promotions are included.
